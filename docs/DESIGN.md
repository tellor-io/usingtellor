## Audit fix decisions — 2026-11-13

- Bridge initialization/reset reject zero voting thresholds. All three validator-set setters reject timestamps strictly above `block.timestamp * 1000`; exact equality remains valid. Zero guardian and zero unbonding seconds are rejected without additional duration bounds. Guardian reset still accepts/emits a checkpoint, now correctly named in the event ABI and NatSpec.
- DataBank validates report/attestation/next-report future bounds and attestation/report ordering before subtraction. Its existing whole-second age/future policy remains; the source readers and Shepherd still reject subsecond-future observation timestamps. Admission failures leave both history arrays unchanged.
- DataBank constructor is `(dataBridge, optimisticDelay, maxAttestationAge, maxDataAge)`, all three durations positive immutable uint256 seconds. No twelve-hour minimum, upper bound, or cross-parameter constraint was added. Age limits remain exclusive; optimistic maturity equality remains accepted. Deployers own dispute safety and timing compatibility; old values survive only as explicit test inputs.
- Both factories use CREATE2 with `keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, userSalt))`. Initialization code binds every constructor argument; same-caller duplicate configurations require distinct salts. The custom Morpho factory additionally registers its instances and emits all per-instance configuration fields. Binding covers initial configuration, not later authorized Shepherd governance or the trustworthiness of dependencies.
- Both custom adapter constructors require a trusted Shepherd factory as their first argument and check source registration there. The Morpho adapter factory fixes that registry at construction. Direct adapters cannot bypass membership checks. Registration authenticates provenance only; deployers still verify the registry and policy. Stock upstream consumers are unchanged.
- The custom Morpho scale exponent is limited to 0–54; final-result overflow and zero checks remain. The ceiling gives roughly `1.16e23` raw-price-ratio headroom at exponent 54, not an unrestricted arithmetic guarantee.

Tests and review are recorded in AUDIT-HANDOFF.md. Constructor/factory/event callers and deployment examples are updated. Current generated artifacts remain untouched; clean artifact rebuilding/retracking and package validation are still required at release.

## Current reads and trust

- Exactly two source slots, `primarySource` and `backupSource`, implement the unchanged `IPriceSource` ABI: `getCurrentValue()`, `queryId()`, and `decimals()`. There is no third diagnostic source or hidden source read path.
- Consumer reads consult only the selected source. Pause blocks reads; missing, malformed, zero-price, zero-timestamp, future-timestamp or over-age data reverts without automatic fallback. Source reverts propagate. Successful native reads preserve raw price and original Unix milliseconds; Shepherd never authors a price or rescales one. Both consumer entry points share one internal pause/selected-source/validity path.
- `queryId` and price precision (0–77 decimals) are immutable. Both sources must report matching metadata. The agreed direction is quote units per whole base unit at that precision; matching metadata does not authenticate source semantics or prices. Implementations are required to keep metadata immutable; malicious dependencies can lie.
- TellorSpotSource and TellorTwapSource own their bank, relay-delay and window configuration. Positive relay delays use strict `relayTimestamp < block.timestamp - delay`; spot delay zero explicitly selects latest. These standalone implementations are unchanged by step four.
- Tellor TWAP arithmetic holds each stored price until the next aggregate timestamp and floors the average over the actual interval, at least the requested window. The endpoint price has zero weight. Its returned interval end does not establish contributor freshness. Two-record intervals, sparse relaying, arbitrary excess duration and long-held prices remain accepted limitations.
- Shepherd enforces one deployment-configurable immutable `MAX_DATA_AGE` in seconds for both sources, comparing age at full millisecond precision. Timestamp validity precedes subtraction; age equal to the limit is valid, age even 1ms above it reverts with `StalePrice`. The same limit applies in both modes, including when both source addresses coincide. Existing sibling adaptors inherit this refusal from Shepherd. Sources can still lie about prices/timestamps; a fresh endpoint is not proof of fresh TWAP contributors or deployment readiness. Sources using the same Tellor stream share its compromise and liveness risks.

## Latest-read output

`latestRoundData()` returns `(uint80 roundId, int256 answer, uint256 startedAt, uint256 updatedAt, uint80 answeredInRound)` directly from Shepherd. It shares `_readSelectedSource()` with native `getCurrentValue()`, reading the selected source once and enforcing pause, price, timestamp and maximum-age checks against the original milliseconds. Only after validation does it reject values above `type(int256).max` with `SignedPriceOverflow`, cast the raw price, and floor the original timestamp to seconds (`timestampMs / 1000`). `decimals()` remains unchanged; no conversion to eight decimals or protocol-specific units occurs. Native reads retain the full `uint256` range and original milliseconds.

`roundId`, `startedAt` and `answeredInRound` are always zero unavailable sentinels. Zero does not name a retrievable round. There is no history, round-start metadata, synthesized progression or `getRoundData`; this is a latest-read subset, not full AggregatorV3 support. Switching may select an older still-valid timestamp, and neither switching nor migration renews observations. Flooring can make a timestamp appear up to 999ms older to a downstream seconds-based age check. It cannot replace original-ms validity checks or make a future report acceptable.


## Current transitions

All six request/execute/cancel actions remain permissionless and work while paused. They validate and compare the same primary and backup reads used by consumers, with primary as the deviation denominator. A delayed backup delays deviation detection: there is no immature-spot bypass. Both sources must pass the common age limit and be usable, including backup at challenge execution; an unavailable backup now prevents switching to it.

| Call | Required comparison |
| --- | --- |
| `requestChallenge()` / `executeChallenge()` | Deviation at each call |
| `requestRecovery()` / `executeRecovery()` | No deviation at each call |
| `cancelChallenge()` | No deviation |
| `cancelRecovery()` | Deviation |

Deviation is strictly greater than the configured fraction of primary, using overflow-safe quotient/remainder arithmetic; threshold equality is in-band. Execution also requires its delay, with equality allowed. Failures preserve mode and pending timers. These are endpoint checks, not continuous-condition proofs: oscillation does not reset an uncancelled timer. Duplicate requests cannot restart it; cancellation followed by a new request starts a fresh timer. Switching preserves pause and its clock, and pending requests do not halt consumer reads.

## Configuration and atomic source migration

The constructor is `(queryId, decimals, primarySource, backupSource, admin, minPauseDuration, configChangeDelay, challengeThresholdBps, [challengeDelaySeconds, recoveryDelaySeconds], maxDataAge, isPausable)`. Factory `Configuration` exposes those fields, with separate challenge/recovery delay fields and one `maxDataAge` field. Threshold is 1–9999 basis points; all core durations use seconds; configuration/challenge/recovery delays and maximum age remain positive, while the initial minimum pause duration accepts any uint256 including zero. Source timing belongs to the readers; observation age policy belongs to Shepherd. `maxDataAge` must be in `1..floor(type(uint256).max / 1000)` seconds or construction reverts with `InvalidMaxAge`. `MAX_DATA_AGE()` returns seconds. Construction validates that multiplication by 1000 fits `uint256`; each checked read then requires `nowMs - timestampMs <= MAX_DATA_AGE * 1000`, after rejecting zero/future timestamps. No public millisecond alias or second age setting is retained.

The human explicitly chose **independent positive challenge delay**. The former `challengeDelay >= ceil(TWAP_WINDOW_MS / 1000)` constraint is removed, and no source timing metadata is added. Deployers/admins must calibrate the immutable challenge and recovery delays against source timing/security requirements at deployment and for every replacement. Generic metadata checks cannot enforce this relationship. A replacement needing different core delays requires a new Shepherd or different source configuration; migration cannot change the existing delays. This policy is not a guarantee of TWAP sampling quality or actual interval duration.

The human chose **one fixed deployment maximum data age** over allowing age changes through delayed paused migration. This fixes freshness policy for an instance; a replacement needing a different limit requires a new Shepherd. Deployers/admins must ensure the common limit accommodates both sources’ relay delays and observation timing. Incompatible timing can make valid source data unreadable as soon as it matures. Generic source metadata cannot prove timing suitability; there is no new source introspection or production calibration default. Explicit test values are test inputs only.

`proposeSourcesChange(newPrimary, newBackup)`, `cancelSourcesChange()`, and `executeSourcesChange()` replace the bank-only migration API. Proposals are admin-only, require the full notice, and cannot overwrite a pending proposal. The pair may retain one current source, but a no-op pair is rejected. Both addresses must have code and compatible identity/precision at proposal and again at execution; these are metadata-only checks, so empty history is permitted. Constructor validation passes expected metadata explicitly to the helper, avoiding constructor-time immutable reads under Solidity 0.8.19.

Execution requires pause only when `isPausable` is true; non-pausable instances execute live. It installs both addresses atomically, clears both pending transition timers, and preserves mode, pause clock and the immutable maximum data age. It never renews a source observation timestamp. Failed execution preserves the old pair and pending proposal. Neither execution nor cancellation needs the old sources to function; cancellation does not consult proposed sources either. Pending source proposals block unpause; pending admin proposals do not. Events are `SourcesChangeProposed`, `SourcesChangeCancelled`, and `SourcesChanged`; getters include `pendingPrimarySource`, `pendingBackupSource`, `sourcesProposedAt`, and `CONFIG_CHANGE_DELAY`.

Consumer `getCurrentValue()` and `queryId()` and transition entry points remain stable; `decimals()` is added. Constructor/factory/migration ABIs change and require fresh deployments, not storage-compatible upgrades. `IBackupOracle` and `StreamBackupOracle` remain standalone examples, not recovery authorities.


## Immutable deployment pausability

The final constructor argument and factory `Configuration` field are `isPausable`, exposed as `bool public immutable isPausable`. True retains existing pause/unpause and paused-migration behavior. False permanently prevents explicit pausing: the existing caller-authorization check runs first, then authorized admin/pauser calls revert with `NotPausable`. The instance stays unpaused with `pausedAt == 0`; admin `unpause()` consequently reverts with `NotPaused`. Admin transfers, memberships and policy changes cannot enable pausing.

The human chose minimal gating over disabling every pause-related administration function. Pauser membership and minimum-pause-duration management keep their current validation, permissions and notice rules, but their settings have no operational effect when `isPausable` is false. Any initially valid pause duration remains accepted, including zero or uint256 max. Source replacement skips only the pause requirement on non-pausable instances: current-admin authority, full notice, metadata rechecks, atomic pair replacement, mode preservation and transition invalidation remain. Reads continue against the selected old source until execution, then use the selected replacement without renewing its observation time. Failed execution preserves the pair, proposal and timers.

Non-pausable does not guarantee price availability: stale/broken sources or a replacement with no usable price can make both consumer entry points revert. Replacement deliberately keeps metadata-only validation and can recover from broken old sources. Source-readiness checks and separate pausable/non-pausable contracts are deferred. The factory creation event records the flag; CREATE2 commits to it through the constructor arguments. Existing constructor/factory positions are retained, with the required bool appended. Updated callers and fresh deployments are required.

## Shared delay and admin transfer

`CONFIG_CHANGE_DELAY` is the one positive immutable notice period in seconds for source-pair migration, admin transfer, minimum-pause-duration updates and threshold updates. It generalizes the former source-only delay, keeping the same constructor positional slot and changing the factory named field to `configChangeDelay`. No second delay or legacy getter alias is retained. Invalid deployment delay reverts with `InvalidConfigChangeDelay`; early execution of these proposal kinds reverts with `ConfigChangeDelayNotElapsed`.

Only the current admin can call `proposeAdminChange(newAdmin)`, `cancelAdminChange()` or `executeAdminChange()`. The recipient never accepts, authorizes or executes the transfer. The destination must be nonzero and different from the current admin; it need not contain code, implement an interface or prove liveness. This deliberately permits relinquishing admin control by transferring to an uncontrollable address, while existing pausers may still pause a pausable instance. There is no separate renounce shortcut.

`pendingAdmin` and `adminProposedAt` expose the proposal. A pending proposal cannot be overwritten; cancellation followed by reproposal starts a fresh full notice. Cancellation/execution without a pending proposal reverts. The outgoing admin executes when elapsed seconds are at least `CONFIG_CHANGE_DELAY`; equality is valid. Execution changes admin and clears only the admin proposal/clock. Events are `AdminChangeProposed(proposedAdmin, proposedAt)`, `AdminChangeCancelled(proposedAdmin)` and `AdminChanged(previousAdmin, newAdmin)`.

All three actions work paused or unpaused. The old admin keeps all authority until execution, then loses admin authority. Separately granted pauser memberships survive; being admin never creates a permanent membership. Source addresses, feed identity/precision, prices, mode, transition requests and their clocks, pause state/clock, and pending source proposal/clock do not change. Source/admin proposal clocks are independent; existing source proposals become governable only by the new admin after transfer. Source migration requires paused execution only on pausable instances and invalidates pending transitions only when that source migration succeeds.

## Mutable minimum pause duration

`minPauseDuration()` is the camel-case getter for mutable policy in uint256 seconds. Constructor/factory `minPauseDuration` initializes it and accepts the full uint256 range including zero. Current-admin-only `proposeMinPauseDurationChange(newDuration)`, `cancelMinPauseDurationChange()` and `executeMinPauseDurationChange()` work paused or unpaused and do not read source data. Proposals equal to the active duration are rejected; a pending proposal cannot be overwritten. Cancel/repropose starts its own new full `CONFIG_CHANGE_DELAY`. Early/unauthorized calls or calls without a pending proposal revert without changing state; delay equality permits execution.

The proposal has `pendingMinPauseDuration`, `minPauseDurationProposedAt` and the explicit boolean `minPauseDurationChangePending`. Zero is a valid proposal, never an absent-proposal sentinel. Events are `MinPauseDurationChangeProposed`, `MinPauseDurationChangeCancelled` and `MinPauseDurationChanged`. Proposal/cancellation leave effective policy and pausedAt unchanged. Execution changes the effective duration immediately and clears only its proposal state. An active pause remains anchored to its original `pausedAt`: unpause requires `block.timestamp - pausedAt >= minPauseDuration`. A decrease can immediately permit unpause, and an increase can extend the wait; zero permits no elapsed-time wait. Execution never unpauses automatically, and admin authority, paused-state requirement and pending-source-proposal restriction still apply.

A pending duration proposal does not block unpause or change the active policy before execution. Updates preserve mode, sources, observations, pause/clock, challenge/recovery requests/clocks, admin/source proposals/clocks and pauser memberships. All four proposal kinds have independent notice periods. Pending duration updates survive admin handover at their original timestamp; only the new current admin can then cancel or execute. No transition requests are invalidated by a duration update because price/deviation policy is unchanged.

## Mutable deviation threshold

`challengeThresholdBps()` is the camel-case getter for mutable policy in uint256 basis points; constructor/factory `challengeThresholdBps` initializes it with the unchanged positional interface. Both deployment and updates require 1–9999. Current-admin-only `proposeChallengeThresholdChange(newThresholdBps)`, `cancelChallengeThresholdChange()` and `executeChallengeThresholdChange()` use the shared immutable positive `CONFIG_CHANGE_DELAY`, with subtraction and equality permitting execution. Invalid/no-op/overwrite/no-pending/unauthorized/early actions revert without state changes. Cancel/repropose starts a fresh notice for this proposal alone.

`pendingChallengeThresholdBps == 0` means absent; `challengeThresholdProposedAt` records the proposal time and clears with the value. Zero is invalid policy, so this representation avoids the redundant boolean an explicit-flag alternative would require. Events are `ChallengeThresholdChangeProposed`, `ChallengeThresholdChangeCancelled` and `ChallengeThresholdChanged` (old/new values). All operations work paused/unpaused without source reads.

Proposal/cancellation preserve active policy and pending transition clocks. Reads and permissionless transition actions continue under the active threshold while a proposal is pending. Execution changes the threshold, clears its proposal, and uses `_invalidatePendingTransitions()` to clear pending challenge/recovery flags and timestamps with their existing invalidation events. Even observations still qualifying under both policies require fresh requests and the full original transition waits. Retaining old timers would reuse delay accumulated under a different comparison policy; the human approved invalidation. The primary-denominator strict rational comparison and overflow-safe arithmetic remain unchanged; threshold equality is in-band.

Execution preserves mode, pause/pausedAt, observations, sources, precision, all other pending configuration values/times and pauser memberships. Pending threshold proposals do not block unpause, including at zero duration; pending source proposals still do. Threshold notices survive admin transfer unchanged, becoming governable only by the new current admin. Source migration independently invalidates transitions while preserving pending threshold policy/notice. Only pause duration and threshold are mutable policies: configuration/challenge/recovery delays, maximum age and feed metadata remain fixed.
